# Interface brief

## Product and moment

What is this? Who is using it, in what situation, and what useful action should they finish?

## Real content and scope

What will actually appear? Include representative long and short content. Distinguish production facts from concept copy. State where the flow begins and ends.

## Intended experience

What should the person feel, and what must the interface do to make that feeling plausible? Translate “premium,” “beautiful,” “simple,” or “playful” into visible or behavioral consequences.

## References and interpretation

For each selected reference, record the source, the specific relationship worth learning from, and what should not be copied. Include image or asset rights where relevant.

## Chosen direction

Describe the composition, the main visual subject, typography, palette, imagery, interaction pacing, and the alternative you rejected. Keep this specific to this product.

## Constraints and unresolved assumptions

Record accessibility needs, supported devices, existing product constraints, asset limitations, and open facts. State reasonable assumptions without representing them as verified facts. Do not turn every preference into a hard rule.

## Delivery

Name the complete flow to implement, the browser states to inspect, and the repository location for code and design decisions. The output is an interface and its supporting decisions, not an aesthetic benchmark.
